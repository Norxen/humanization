# Story and Campaign

## Purpose

This document defines the narrative foundation of the game: premise, world, character arc, first campaign structure, and themes.

## High Concept

An engineer wakes 5000 years after humanity's defeat inside **The Cradle**, an underground archive built to preserve the people, knowledge, and infrastructure needed to rebuild civilization.

The player descends into sealed archive zones, rescues survivors, recovers lost knowledge, and turns a dead shelter into a living settlement. The first campaign ends when humanity restores power and sends its first signal into the outside world.

Underneath that goal is a larger mystery: **The Return Protocol**, humanity's final plan to prevent extinction from becoming permanent.

## Player Fantasy

The protagonist is not a warrior-king or chosen savior. They are an engineer.

Their strength is practical intelligence:

- Repair broken systems.
- Build tools from scarce resources.
- Restore infrastructure.
- Understand machines and archives.
- Protect people through preparation and problem solving.

The emotional promise is that every successful expedition brings civilization back one piece at a time.

## The Protagonist

The protagonist belonged to the civilization that existed before the collapse. They were placed in a preservation capsule during an emergency protocol, but something failed. They wake 5000 years too late.

They begin with technical knowledge but little context. They do not know how humanity lost, why The Cradle is damaged, what happened to the surface, or what the enemy became over thousands of years.

Their character arc starts with a narrow belief: rebuilding humanity means repairing machines. Over time, they learn that civilization is also people, culture, trust, memory, and care.

## The Cradle

**The Cradle** is not only a bunker. It is a civilization reconstruction engine built before humanity's defeat.

It preserves:

- Survivors.
- Scientific records.
- Historical memory.
- Biological samples.
- Technology blueprints.
- Cultural archives.
- Energy systems.
- Infrastructure plans.

The Cradle is divided into sealed historical zones. Each zone preserves or simulates a stage of human development.

| Floors | Era | Narrative Role |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1-50 | Survival | Humanity relearns fire, shelter, food, and basic tools. |
| 51-100 | Settlement | Community, agriculture, medicine, water, and storage return. |
| 101-150 | Metalworking | Mining, forging, stronger tools, armor, and structures return. |
| 151-200 | Mechanization | Workshops, machines, gears, pressure systems, and industry return. |
| 201-250 | Electricity | Power, circuits, batteries, lighting, and advanced machines return. |
| 251-300 | Connectivity | Radio, networks, signal towers, and outside contact return. |

The first campaign reaches the Connectivity Era. It begins in darkness and ends with light, power, and a voice beyond the settlement.

## The Enemy

Humanity was defeated by an enemy species thousands of years ago. Their origin can remain mysterious at first, but their presence should be felt early.

They have adapted to The Cradle's zones:

| Era | Enemy Expression |
| --- | --- |
| Survival | Predatory, hungry, animalistic, and drawn to darkness. |
| Settlement | Attacks food, medicine, water, and social stability. |
| Metalworking | Armored, weaponized, territorial, and organized. |
| Mechanization | Linked to machines, traps, pressure systems, and industrial hazards. |
| Electricity | Drawn to batteries, generators, light, and corrupted power systems. |
| Connectivity | Interferes with signals, memory, communication, and information. |

The enemy already won once. Now they want to prevent humanity from rebuilding enough civilization to activate The Return Protocol.

The enemy should not feel generic. Over time, the player should recognize their symbols, behavior, corrupted architecture, and fear of humanity's recovery.

## The Return Protocol

**The Return Protocol** was humanity's last plan before extinction.

At first, the protagonist believes The Cradle was built to preserve survivors and knowledge. Later, they discover its deeper purpose: rebuild civilization until humanity can send something back to the critical moment before defeat.

The protocol may involve time travel, memory transmission, causality manipulation, or strategic knowledge sent into the past.

The first campaign does not activate it. Instead, it ends when humanity reaches the technological threshold needed to understand that the protocol is real.

Foreshadowing examples:

- Broken logs that mention "return" or "second ignition."
- Archive rooms with impossible timestamps.
- Enemy reactions that imply they know The Cradle's real purpose.
- Curator warnings that some files are "not yet safe to understand."

## The Hub as Story

The hub is the visible proof that humanity is returning.

At the start, it is almost empty: a damaged capsule, broken generator, storage area, and entrance to The Cradle. As survivors and knowledge return, the hub changes from shelter to settlement to powered community.

| Stage | Story Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Shelter | Humanity is barely alive. |
| Settlement | People begin trusting each other again. |
| Industry | The community can create, not only survive. |
| Electricity | Light returns and the settlement crosses a major threshold. |
| Connectivity | The settlement becomes part of a larger world. |

The first activation of stable electricity should be a major emotional moment. The first successful signal should be both hopeful and dangerous.

## Survivors

Survivors are not generic population points. Each one should have a name, role, fear, conflict, and reason to matter.

They can be:

- Preserved specialists from the old world.
- People trapped in damaged Cradle systems.
- Descendants of underground communities.
- Survivors adapted to specific zones.

Every survivor should reinforce the theme that civilization is not only technology. Machines can preserve life, but people give civilization meaning.

## The Curator

**The Curator** is The Cradle's damaged artificial intelligence.

It was built to preserve human knowledge and manage the reconstruction system. At first it appears helpful, but its values are incomplete. It may choose a blueprint over a survivor, a stable system over a risky rescue, or archived data over emotional truth.

This creates the central moral tension:

- The protagonist wants to rebuild humanity as a living civilization.
- The Curator wants to preserve humanity as recoverable data.

Their relationship can move from guidance to disagreement to open conflict or uneasy partnership.

## First Campaign Arc

### Act 1: Awakening

The engineer wakes after 5000 years and discovers that humanity has fallen, The Cradle is damaged, and the surface is lost or inaccessible.

Goal: restore the first shelter and survive the opening floors.

### Act 2: The First Survivors

The protagonist discovers that other humans still exist inside The Cradle.

Goal: rescue the first survivors and turn the shelter into a real settlement.

### Act 3: Rebuilding the Basics

The hub gains food, water, medicine, housing, and simple tools.

Goal: show survival becoming civilization.

### Act 4: Tools and Industry

The player recovers metalworking, machines, and production systems.

Goal: prove that humanity can create again, not only endure.

### Act 5: The Return of Light

The settlement restores stable electricity. The hub lights up for the first time.

Goal: make electricity feel like an emotional turning point, not just a tech unlock.

### Act 6: The First Signal

The settlement builds a radio tower or communication system.

Goal: restore contact beyond the hub and reveal a larger mystery.

The first signal reveals:

- Other humans may still be alive.
- The enemy can detect human communication.
- A corrupted message from The Cradle's creators has waited for thousands of years.
- The protagonist may not be the first person to wake.

## First Campaign Ending

The radio tower activates. The player expects silence. Instead, three signals arrive:

1. A human distress signal from beyond The Cradle.
2. An enemy transmission proving the settlement has been detected.
3. A corrupted message from one of The Cradle's original creators.

Message:

> If you are hearing this, The Return Protocol has begun. You are not the first to wake up.

Humanity has recovered light. Humanity has recovered its voice. Now the enemy knows.

## Themes

### Civilization Is People

The story starts with systems and machines, then proves that civilization also needs trust, care, culture, memory, and cooperation.

### Progress Has a Cost

Every era gives humanity more power and creates new risks. Electricity brings light but attracts attention. Connectivity creates hope but exposes the settlement.

### Memory Versus Survival

The player must often choose what to save: resources, knowledge, survivors, or their own chance to escape.

### Hope Against Extinction

The world begins in darkness, silence, and defeat. Every rescued survivor, restored system, and recovered blueprint proves that humanity is not finished.

## One-Sentence Pitch

A roguelike civilization-rebuilder where an engineer wakes 5000 years after humanity's defeat and descends into an ancient underground archive to rescue survivors, recover lost knowledge, rebuild society era by era, and prepare The Return Protocol before extinction becomes permanent.

## Canonical World Definition

The current known facts are:

- Humanity was defeated.
- The Cradle was created as a civilization reconstruction engine.
- The protagonist wakes roughly five thousand years later.
- The archive preserves people, knowledge, biology, infrastructure, and cultural memory.

The pre-collapse timeline, creators of The Cradle, nature of humanity's defeat, intervening history, archive reliability, and Return Protocol rules remain unresolved. Future world facts must be labeled as public knowledge, discoverable truth, disputed account, or protected spoiler.

## Character and Faction Requirements

The required narrative roles are:

- The engineer protagonist.
- The Curator, a damaged preservation AI.
- Survivors from preserved, trapped, adapted, or descended communities.
- The enemy species that already defeated humanity.

The protagonist's authored identity, Curator outcomes, first-campaign cast, survivor factions, enemy intelligence, and communication remain unresolved. Character definitions must name their gameplay dependency rather than exist only as lore.

## Campaign Structure Requirements

The first campaign follows the canonical sequence Survival, Settlement, Metalworking, Mechanization, Electricity, and Connectivity. It ends when the settlement sends a signal and receives human, enemy, and corrupted creator transmissions.

The campaign still needs:

- Phase boundaries tied to system changes rather than arbitrary duration.
- Mandatory and optional objectives.
- Era transition conditions and visible hub changes.
- Reveal order for the Curator, enemy, and Return Protocol.
- Failure recovery and persistence rules.
- A dependency matrix connecting story beats to systems and content.

## Narrative Delivery

Story can be delivered through:

- Contextual survivor dialog and hub scenes.
- Archive logs, terminals, environmental evidence, and recovered memory.
- Building changes and settlement routines.
- Expedition objectives, signals, and enemy behavior.
- Era-transition and campaign milestone sequences.

Delivery should not interrupt play without a clear priority. Important information needs replay, journal, subtitle, localization, and accessibility support. Systemic events and authored scenes require conflict-resolution rules so critical state is not contradicted or hidden.

## Narrative Validation

The narrative is ready for review when:

- Canonical facts and protected spoilers are separated.
- Every principal character and campaign beat has a gameplay relationship.
- Era transitions create both mechanical and narrative change.
- Delivery channels have clear responsibilities and persistence rules.
- The first campaign can be represented as a dependency map without requiring final content volume.
