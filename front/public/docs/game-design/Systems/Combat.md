# Combat

## Purpose

Combat should be mechanically satisfying without turning the engineer into a generic action hero. Direct weapon use creates immediate skill expression; scanning, tools, gadgets, positioning, and environmental control let the player reshape encounters and resolve them more efficiently.

Combat is one response to danger. Avoidance, disabling machinery, creating a path, spending a tool, or leaving an optional reward behind must remain valid decisions.

## Combat Identity

The game uses real-time top-down or isometric action with independent movement and free aim.

The target experience is:

> Read the danger, create an advantage, execute precisely, and preserve enough capability to continue the expedition.

Encounters favor small groups of dangerous, readable enemies rather than large hordes. Ordinary threats remain manageable with responsive weapons, while correct engineering decisions shorten fights, reduce resource cost, and create safer outcomes.

## Combat Pillars

### Mechanical Skill Matters

Aiming, movement, evade timing, target priority, positioning, and committed tool deployment must feel responsive and learnable.

### Engineering Changes the Fight

The scanner reveals opportunities. Tools and gadgets create control, expose vulnerabilities, redirect enemies, manipulate machinery, and turn environmental systems into advantages.

### Correct Play Resolves Threats Faster

Enemies have moderate default durability. Weak points, status interactions, control effects, environmental hazards, and correct tool use produce fast resolutions instead of only increasing numerical damage.

### Resources Create Expedition Pressure

The sidearm and engineering tool remain dependable. Primary ammunition or energy, healing, and powerful gadgets are limited enough that each encounter affects later decisions.

### Technology Evolves Without Invalidating Skill

Each civilization era adds or transforms combat verbs. Earlier weapons and tools remain usable through repair, modification, combination, and new interactions.

## Standard Field Loadout

| Slot | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Primary Weapon | Strong or specialized weapon using limited ammunition, charge, or energy. |
| Sidearm | Dependable fallback governed by reload, heat, or renewable ammunition rather than scarce expedition stock. |
| Engineering Tool | Multifunction device for scanning, interaction, repair, hacking, and era-specific operations. |
| Gadget Slots | Two limited devices chosen before the expedition or changed through supported field systems. |
| Healing | Limited recovery that competes with other expedition supplies. |

Exact weapon archetypes, quantities, timings, and upgrade trees remain separate content and tuning decisions.

## Combat Rhythm

```text
Observe or scan
-> Identify threats, weak points, and environment links
-> Position and choose an engagement
-> Create or expose a vulnerability
-> Exploit it with weapon skill, a tool, or the environment
-> Resolve the immediate threat
-> Reassess health, ammunition, gadgets, and position
```

The scanner supports this rhythm without pausing combat. Device placement is quick but committed: the player aims a preview, releases to deploy, and remains vulnerable during a short placement action.

## Validation Criteria

The combat direction is successful when:

- Basic movement, free aim, firing, and evasion are enjoyable without gadgets.
- Scanning and engineering create meaningful options rather than mandatory busywork.
- Correct exploitation produces visibly faster and safer outcomes.
- Encounters remain readable with several distinct threats active.
- Resource use affects expedition decisions without making ordinary combat feel unusable.
- Era upgrades add interactions and combinations rather than only increasing damage.
