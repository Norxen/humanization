# Era Progression

## Canonical First-Campaign Sequence

The first campaign uses six civilization eras:

1. Survival
2. Settlement
3. Metalworking
4. Mechanization
5. Electricity
6. Connectivity

Modern and Future technology may exist in preserved or experimental forms, but they are not civilization eras in the current first-campaign progression.

## Era Meaning

An era represents sustainable settlement capability, not the oldest or most advanced object the player has found.

The current era determines what the community can understand, repair, power, supply, reproduce, integrate, and improve. Individual buildings can advance unevenly, but the settlement still needs enough connected capability to support major transitions.

## Capability-Gated Salvage

Advanced relics can appear before their supporting era.

| Salvage State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Unknown | The settlement cannot identify the object's function or risk. |
| Understood | The function is known, but operation or repair remains unsupported. |
| Unreliable | The relic can operate with limited charges, instability, or irreplaceable components. |
| Supported | The settlement can repair, power, and supply it. |
| Reproducible | The settlement can manufacture or consistently rebuild it. |
| Integrated | The technology combines with buildings, tools, networks, and new upgrade paths. |

This lets discovery create aspiration without allowing isolated loot to bypass civilization rebuilding.

## Combat Capability by Era

### Survival

- Improvised melee and ranged weapons.
- Scavenged sidearms with uncertain maintenance.
- Manual traps and simple mechanical devices.
- Basic scanner identification.
- Advanced salvage is fragile, poorly understood, or effectively irreplaceable.

### Settlement

- Standardized repairs and shared equipment practices.
- Repeatable basic ammunition and dependable mechanical weapons.
- Field support equipment and constructed cover.
- Stable production of simple traps and utility gadgets.
- Specialists begin maintaining recovered technology consistently.

### Metalworking

- Precision components and durable weapon construction.
- Reliable firearms, blades, armor penetration, and reinforced equipment.
- Stronger weak-point exploitation through specialized ammunition and tools.
- Better protection, repair quality, and reusable device housings.

### Mechanization

- Automated turrets and mechanically assisted weapons.
- Pneumatic, pressure, and motion-driven systems.
- Advanced barricades and deployable battlefield control.
- Combat interaction with moving machinery, pumps, gears, and industrial hazards.
- Devices begin performing sustained actions after deployment.

### Electricity

- Powered tools, sensors, and enhanced scanner functions.
- Shock, overload, conduction, and energy-based control effects.
- Electrical weapons and powered defensive equipment.
- Batteries, generators, and circuits create new dependencies and vulnerabilities.
- Devices can link through local powered systems.

### Connectivity

- Networked gadgets and coordinated drones.
- Remote hacking and shared targeting information.
- Linked device behaviors and distributed sensing.
- Signal warfare, interference, and remote system control.
- Combat capability depends on information integrity as well as physical power.

## Progression Rule

Each era should add or transform combat interactions:

```text
Manual
-> Standardized
-> Precise
-> Automated
-> Powered
-> Networked
```

Older equipment remains relevant through repair, modification, combination, reduced operating cost, or interactions unlocked by later capability. Era progression must not become a sequence of replacing familiar weapons with numerically larger versions.

## Transition Requirements

Every era transition must eventually define:

- Settlement capabilities and buildings.
- Required knowledge and specialists.
- Resource and production dependencies.
- Dungeon access or milestone conditions.
- Combat actions and interactions added or transformed.
- Enemy and hazard responses.
- Visible and emotional settlement changes.

Exact transition gates, bosses, floor requirements, and content quantities remain unresolved.

## Validation Criteria

The era model is ready for review when every transition demonstrates:

- A meaningful new civilization capability.
- At least one transformed existing system.
- A sustainable production or support dependency.
- New combat and dungeon interactions.
- Continued relevance for earlier resources and equipment.
- A visible settlement and campaign consequence.
