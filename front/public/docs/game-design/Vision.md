# Vision

## Purpose

This document defines what the game is trying to make the player feel, the promises every system must support, and the boundaries used to reject unfocused features. The working title is **Roguelike Civilization Rebuilder**.

## Vision Statement

The player is an engineer who repeatedly enters a dangerous underground archive, secures people, resources, and knowledge, and uses them to rebuild a visible human civilization era by era.

## Core Fantasy

Be the engineer who brings humanity back from extinction by understanding broken systems, recovering what was lost, and making difficult expeditions serve a living community.

## Design Pillars

### Rebuild Through Risk

Dungeon danger matters because the player carries value that can change the settlement. Hub progress matters because it prepares the player to accept greater danger.

### Civilization Is Visible

Progress must alter spaces, routines, available actions, survivor behavior, and the settlement's emotional tone. Advancement cannot exist only as larger numbers.

### Preparation Is Power

The protagonist succeeds through engineering, planning, tools, knowledge, and informed execution. Mechanical mastery should reward understanding rather than raw character strength alone.

### Extraction Creates Tension

Rewards are not permanent until extraction. Choosing whether to leave or continue is the central risk decision of a run.

### People Give Progress Meaning

Survivors are characters and capabilities, not population currency. Technology restores possibility; people turn it into civilization.

## Experience Goals

- Enter each run with a concrete settlement need.
- Read spaces and threats before committing resources.
- Express skill through movement, aim, timing, positioning, and engineering.
- Feel increasing tension as unsecured value grows.
- Return to a hub that visibly responds to success and failure.
- Understand why each technology, survivor, and resource matters.
- Experience civilization milestones as mechanical and emotional events.

## Scope

The complete-game model includes:

- Repeatable dungeon expeditions with meaningful extraction decisions.
- Real-time isometric combat with independent aim and engineering control.
- A walkable, evolving civilization hub.
- Survivor rescue, assignment, requests, and consequences.
- Resource, knowledge, equipment, and era progression.
- A campaign about restoring humanity and uncovering the Return Protocol.

## Non-Goals

- A full city simulation where every citizen has a complete daily life model.
- Loot collection disconnected from settlement needs.
- Permanent failure that casually deletes secured civilization progress.
- A power fantasy centered on the protagonist becoming a warrior-king.
- Large hordes as the default source of combat difficulty.
- Defining the game through arbitrary early-build content counts before its dependencies are understood.

## Review Rules

A proposed feature must strengthen a design pillar, close an important dependency, or satisfy a later production milestone. Features that only add volume, simulation detail, or numerical progression without improving the core loop should be rejected or deferred.

The vision is ready for review when the intended platforms and input methods are selected and playtest language exists for combat skill, extraction tension, hub payoff, and the first major era transition.
