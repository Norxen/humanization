# Content Requirements

## Purpose

Define what each content family must express and what it costs to produce before choosing rosters or volume. System rules remain in Systems; this document owns reusable content schemas and variation requirements.

## Enemies

The enemy species adapts to each Cradle era and threatens the systems humanity is rebuilding. Enemy design should reveal behavior, history, and escalating strategic intent.

Combat normally presents small groups of dangerous, readable enemies with complementary roles. Enemies should have moderate default durability and faster resolution paths created by weak points, control effects, positioning, environmental interactions, or engineering tools.

Required enemy schema:

- Role and tactical purpose.
- Senses, movement, attacks, coordination, and retreat behavior.
- Durability, protection, weak points, and status interactions.
- Engineering and environmental responses.
- Era expression, rewards, narrative function, and production dependencies.

Open questions include core roles, species taxonomy, shared readability, group synergies, AI complexity, and reuse across eras.

## Bosses

Bosses are optional system and campaign tools, not a mandatory quota. A boss must justify its production cost by testing a major mastery point, expressing an era or enemy adaptation, changing progression, or advancing the campaign.

Define retry and extraction behavior, encounter phases, unique rewards, permanent world changes, and reuse expectations before selecting a roster.

## Items and Equipment

The standard combat contract reserves one primary weapon, one dependable sidearm, one engineering tool, two gadget slots, and limited healing.

Required item schema:

- Slot and gameplay responsibility.
- Crafted, recovered, repaired, or unique source.
- Era capability needed to power, supply, repair, reproduce, and upgrade it.
- Ammunition, energy, heat, durability, or charge model.
- Interactions with combat, environment, inventory, and hub production.
- Visual, animation, audio, and balancing dependencies.

Rarity and affixes should only exist if they improve decisions without undermining authored roles or capability-gated salvage.

## Locations and Biomes

Cradle locations combine era, function, threat, resources, history, and spatial identity.

Required location schema:

- Era and theme tags.
- Critical route and optional-space requirements.
- Compatible enemies, hazards, resources, objectives, and landmarks.
- Environmental interactions and engineering opportunities.
- Reuse limits, variation rules, narrative opportunities, and presentation cost.

Generation terminology must remain consistent with the six canonical eras. Location families should be selected by systemic coverage and reuse value, not by an arbitrary biome count.

## Survivors and NPCs

Every authored survivor should have a name, role, fear, conflict, and reason to matter. Assignment begins as a capability unlock rather than a deep workforce simulation.

Required survivor schema:

- Identity, role, and hub function.
- Rescue or recruitment context.
- Assignment requirements and temporary states.
- Relationships, conflicts, reactions, and campaign dependencies.
- Authored versus systemic dialog requirements.
- Injury, recovery, departure, and loss constraints.

The balance between authored principal characters and reusable systemic survivors remains unresolved.

## Completion Criteria

Content requirements are ready for review when each family has a canonical schema, dependency estimate, reuse strategy, validation method, and representative examples sufficient for the Complexity Map. Final counts remain an MVP or campaign planning decision.
