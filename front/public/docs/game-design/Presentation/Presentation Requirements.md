# Presentation Requirements

## UI and UX

The player should understand the current hub need, run objective, carried unsecured value, extraction opportunity, and expected payoff without navigating unrelated menus.

Combat UI must support independent movement and free aim, show two sequentially recovering evade charges, communicate scanner recovery, distinguish primary and sidearm state, and preview valid engineering or gadget placement without obscuring threats.

Required definition:

- Platform and input targets.
- HUD density and diegetic presentation.
- Hub, expedition board, inventory, upgrade, and journal flows.
- Tutorial and first-time-use strategy.
- Controller focus and responsive layouts.
- Free-aim controller assistance and reticle behavior.

## Visual Direction

The visual language must distinguish preserved humanity, decay, enemy corruption, and active rebuilding. Each civilization era needs readable materials, silhouettes, lighting, and hub transformation without requiring every asset to be unique.

Required definition:

- Camera projection, framing, occlusion, and readability constraints.
- Character, enemy, weak-point, hazard, and interactable readability.
- Era palette and material evolution.
- Hub construction and milestone presentation.
- Reuse strategy, asset quality bar, animation needs, and performance constraints.

## Audio Direction

Audio must prioritize combat telegraphs, hazards, objective state, extraction, and critical hub changes before ambience or musical detail.

Required definition:

- Sonic identities for The Cradle, the enemy, and civilization eras.
- Feedback priority and masking rules.
- Hub ambience as population and infrastructure return.
- Adaptive music across risk and milestones.
- Voice, synthetic speech, localization, subtitles, and asset budget.

## Accessibility

Accessibility requirements apply from the start:

- Complete remapping and controller support.
- Adjustable free-aim assistance while preserving independent targeting as the default.
- Text size, contrast, color-independent meaning, and readable diagrams.
- Subtitles, captions, directional-sound alternatives, and separate volume categories.
- Motion, flashing, camera shake, and timing adjustments.
- Difficulty assists that reduce execution barriers without hiding their system effects.

Target standards, test devices, and assistive scenarios remain to be selected. Accessibility acceptance criteria must be attached to each later production milestone.

## Completion Criteria

Presentation requirements are ready for review when the project has task flows, information hierarchy, visual and audio theses, readability rules, accessibility targets, representative quality bars, and dependency estimates for the Complexity Map.
